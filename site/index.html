Windows treats every monitor the same. DispCtrl treats each one on its own terms: brightness in step across every screen, night light, keep awake, a taskbar that hides where you want it, OLED care and your monitor's own settings.

The headline act

One slider. Every display. In step.

Calibrate each monitor's range once, and a laptop panel and an external monitor match at every level after that. External monitors are driven over DDC/CI and built-in screens through Windows, with software dimming below the panel's own floor.

Turn on Replace Windows brightness and the Quick Settings slider and your laptop's brightness keys move every display, not just the one they were made for.

Quick panel

One click from the taskbar

Click the tray icon and the panel rises from the taskbar, like Windows' own Quick Settings, and sinks back when you click away.

  • One slider for every display, and a row for each one
  • Tiles for night light, dark mode, focus, keep awake, stay active, the taskbar and OLED care
  • Options behind every tile's arrow, so nothing needs digging for
  • Yours to arrange: choose which sections, tiles and rows appear

Command line

Every feature,
scriptable

dispctrl does everything the app does, with JSON output and meaningful exit codes. Use it in scripts, Task Scheduler and your own shortcuts.

  • Per display or all at once: brightness, night light, modes, inputs
  • Monitor controls by name, including ones the device library maps
  • Talks to the running engine, or works on its own
Windows Terminal — dispctrl
# every connected monitor
> dispctrl displays list
1  Internal 2880x1800   2  DELL U2424H (primary)

# every display to 60%, in step
> dispctrl unison set --level 60

# switch the Dell to HDMI 1
> dispctrl display set --monitor 2 --controls input-source=hdmi-1

# keep the screen on and stay Available
> dispctrl awake set --stay-active on
Open source

Built in the open,
with the community

Every monitor is a little different, and no one person owns them all. DispCtrl learns each one it meets, and a shared, open device library carries what one owner works out to everyone with the same model.

01
Plug in any monitor

DispCtrl reads what it supports over DDC/CI and remembers the model.

02
Name what the maker didn't

Watch the undocumented codes change as you use the monitor's menu, give them names, or say what a laptop panel is.

03
Contribute it

One click opens a GitHub issue. After review it joins the library, and every owner of that model gets it.

No telemetry, ever. Sharing is always your choice, and serial numbers are removed before you see the text.
